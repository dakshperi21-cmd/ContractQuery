Our story

From a junior-year econ class to a platform for everyone.

1
2025 — Chantilly, Virginia
An unexpected interest in economics

During his junior year at Chantilly High School, Daksh took an economics class that changed how he saw the world — supply and demand, incentives, opportunity cost weren't abstract theory, they were the hidden logic behind everyday decisions. The more he learned, the more obvious a gap became: most people never get taught these ideas at all, and the ones who do usually learn them as exam material, not as something useful for their own lives.

2
2025 — Discovering prediction markets
Kalshi, Polymarket, and a fantasy football group chat

The idea took shape somewhere unexpected: chatting with friends about their fantasy football league. Someone brought up Kalshi and Polymarket — sites where people trade on whether real-world events will happen. Daksh started digging in and found something more interesting than the betting itself: every contract was economics playing out live, a constantly moving number showing exactly what a large group of people collectively believed. Prediction markets weren't just a curiosity — they were the clearest, most hands-on illustration of the concepts from that econ class he'd found yet. He decided to build something that put that illustration in front of as many people as possible.

3
Late 2025 — Building Probable
Probable is built

Probable started as a simple way to look up a Polymarket contract without already knowing the platform. It grew from there: a market browser so people who didn't know what to search for could still explore; a glossary and an ELI5 mode so the numbers came with plain-language explanations instead of jargon; and starter questions to turn a page of odds into an actual conversation.

4
2026 — Today, still growing
Making it accessible everywhere

The goal now is the same one that started this: make foundational financial literacy something every student, educator, and curious adult can access — regardless of the econ class they did or didn't happen to take. That means continuing to build out features like ELI5 mode, growing the glossary, and working toward deeper AI-powered guidance that meets learners wherever they're starting from — reaching classrooms, businesses, and individual learners across the country, not just the people lucky enough to sit in the right classroom.

The founder
DP
Daksh Peri
Founder, Probable

Daksh is a student at Chantilly High School in Chantilly, Virginia, and an economics enthusiast. He founded Probable after seeing firsthand how few of his peers had access to foundational financial literacy — and after discovering, through prediction markets, just how directly economics could be made to feel real. Probable is his attempt to turn that same discovery into something anyone can access, no econ class required.
